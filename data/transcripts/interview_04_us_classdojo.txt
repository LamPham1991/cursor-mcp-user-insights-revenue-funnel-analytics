Q: What do you care most about when sending your child to school?
A: "That she is going to be set up for success in the classroom and that her
   teachers are partners in her success."

Q: How do you keep track of your child's studies?
A: "You can look in the app under the specified classroom."

Q: What are your biggest challenges when tracking your child's academic
   progress?
A: "The infrequency of progress reports, especially when the teacher is not
   inputting the progress reports in a timely manner."

Q: How often do you feel the need to contact your child's teacher about their
   academic performance or behavior?
A: "Once a day, every day, with a breakdown of performance and behavior, along
   with auxiliary notes from the teachers."

Q: How do you currently receive information about your child's progress at
   school? What are the primary ways you communicate with your child's teachers?
A: "Via the ClassDojo app updates."

Q: How satisfied are you with the current communication tools provided by your
   child's school? (1–5, 1 = disappointed, 5 = very satisfied). Why?
A: "2. The paywall is quite upsetting since I believe it should be the
   teachers' responsibility to pay for it rather than the parents."

Q: How satisfied are you with these methods of communication? (1–5)
A: "2, because I can't save any of the messages that come through, and message
   sending is delayed."

Q: Can you share any recent experiences where you found it challenging to
   communicate with a teacher?
A: "I attempted to contact the principal via ClassDojo only to find out they
   don't actually even use the app."

Q: What made this situation difficult for you?
A: "The lack of availability of the principal for the question to be answered."

Q: How do you typically collaborate with teachers to support your child's
   learning?
A: "I'm supposed to be receiving updates every 30 minutes, which come as a
   daily report at the end of the day."

Q: Are there specific ways you feel this collaboration could be improved?
A: "Receiving updates in a timely manner."

Q: How often do you communicate with your child's teachers? Is this frequency
   sufficient, or would you prefer more or less communication?
A: "Once a day is good."

Q: How important is it for you to receive timely updates from the teachers?
   (1–5). Can you give an example of a time when timing of communication was
   an issue?
A: "5. It was an issue for her last year when the child's teacher was not
   frequent with the updates, and her child started lagging behind because of
   it."

Q: What tools or platforms do you currently use to communicate with teachers?
   Any you dislike or avoid?
A: "ClassDojo."

Q: Any tools/platforms you like and why?
A: "I love the weekly coverage of the curriculum."

Q: If you could change one thing about how you communicate with teachers, what
   would it be?
A: "I wish you could send parent-teacher conference calendar links and receive
   more regular updates on the kids. I also wish teachers could set reminders
   for kids."

Q: What type of information do you most need or value from teachers (e.g.
   academic progress, behavior updates, upcoming events)?
A: "Testing reminders, updates for students, and events in school."

KEY TAKEAWAYS (from source doc):

- The interviewee currently uses ClassDojo for communication.
- The biggest concerns are (a) having to pay to see a full breakdown of the
  child's performance, and (b) issues arising when teachers and staff do not
  fully utilize the app to keep in contact with parents.
- This suggests that the focus should be on building an app for teachers
  rather than solely for parents. It's essential to consider how to
  incentivize teachers to engage with the app.
- The interviewee prefers receiving updates via SMS rather than navigating
  through an app to find necessary information.
