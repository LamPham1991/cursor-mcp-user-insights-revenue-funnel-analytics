[Interviewer]: Hello, hello, hello — speaking clearly.
[Participant]: Clear as day. Ok, fine.

[Participant]:
Let [Interviewer] just ask quickly since you're working, right?

[Interviewer]:
Yeah, no worries. My team at the bootcamp is doing surveys and interviews with
parents who send their kids to school — the kids are quite young, from
elementary school and below. We want to ask some questions and ask for your
permission to record today's session so we can use the transcript to compile
data and create a feature set. Is that ok?

[Participant]:
Sure, go ahead.

[Interviewer]:
When did you and your spouse start sending your kid to school?

[Participant]:
We started when the kid was 15 months old.

[Interviewer]:
How long has your kid been going to school now?

[Participant]:
About four and a half, almost five months.

[Interviewer]:
How did you feel when your child started going to school?

[Participant]:
Oh, since they are cooperating well, everything feels normal and peaceful. No
illness or anything, and they seem happy, so it's all ok.

[Interviewer]:
Private or public school?

[Participant]:
Private. Very close to our house.

[Interviewer]:
What are the main concerns you have when sending your child to school?

[Participant]:
Well, since they are so young, the first concern is cleanliness, then the food,
and then sleep. Finally, the ratio of one teacher to three kids is ideal.

[Interviewer]:
Do you mean the number of students in a class?

[Participant]:
No, the teacher-to-kid ratio. For example, if there are three teachers, there
should be a maximum of nine kids. If two teachers, then six kids. Smaller groups
help to avoid illnesses.

[Interviewer]:
How many kids in your child's class right now?

[Participant]:
Currently, there are two teachers and five kids, so it's within a reasonable
number.

[Interviewer]:
How do you usually get the information you care about?

[Participant]:
We ask directly at the school or meet with the teachers.

[Interviewer]:
Do you meet and ask the teacher directly before starting school?

[Participant]:
Yes, we do a school tour and gather information.

[Interviewer]:
Do you still check or inquire daily when your child comes back home from
school?

[Participant]:
We research thoroughly in the beginning, so we feel assured and let them go to
school. There is also a camera.

[Interviewer]:
A camera in the school's app or somewhere else?

[Participant]:
Yes, the school has an app that integrates a camera and communicates with
parents.

[Interviewer]:
What's the name of that app?

[Participant]:
Let me check. It's "Sound & Kits."

[Interviewer]:
Is that the school's app?

[Participant]:
It's a third-party app that provides services to the school.

[Interviewer]:
In the app, do you just use the camera feature, or are there other actions?

[Participant]:
Many things — viewing camera footage, receiving notifications from the school
and teachers, group chats, etc.

[Interviewer]:
Is there daily attendance tracking? Do they upload the menu or class
activities on the app?

[Participant]:
Yes, everything is available on the app.

[Interviewer]:
Can you communicate directly with the teacher through the app?

[Participant]:
Yes, there's a chat feature with the teacher.

[Interviewer]:
Is it a messaging system where you send a message and the teacher replies
whenever they see it, or is it more real-time?

[Participant]:
Usually, you send a message and the teacher responds when they check the app.
It doesn't have any real-time notifications.

[Interviewer]:
How would you rate the app's ease of use on a scale from 1 to 5?

[Participant]:
Around 4. It's in Vietnamese, so it's easy to use.

[Interviewer]:
What's missing for it to reach a 5?

[Participant]:
Stability. Sometimes the camera function isn't very good, and it crashes
occasionally.

[Interviewer]:
How often do you use the app?

[Participant]:
Mainly to check the camera and see notifications.
